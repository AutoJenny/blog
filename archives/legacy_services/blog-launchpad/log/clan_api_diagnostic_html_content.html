What Makes the Triskelion Unique? A Deep Dive into its Symbolism

Published on December 15, 2025 | By Caitrin Stewart

Ancient Symbolism Awakens

The triskelion, a symbol of ancient Celtic significance, can be found etched into the stones of Scotland's rugged landscape. This curious emblem has captivated the imagination of scholars and enthusiasts alike, its meaning shrouded in mystery and intrigue.

Across Celtic cultures, the triskelion is woven into various forms and interpretations. In Ireland, it's associated with the mythological god Lugh, said to embody strength, magic, and craftsmanship. Similarly, in Scotland, the triskelion appears on ancient artifacts, such as the Brochs of Clackmannanshire, symbolising protection, courage, and resilience.

Wales and Cornwall also boast their own unique takes on the triskelion, often blended with Christian influences. The Cornish triskelion, for instance, is sometimes depicted alongside three spirals, signifying eternal life and spiritual growth. In Scotland's north-east, the triskelion adorns ancient standing stones, like the one at Rolla Hill, near Montrose.

Despite its variations, the triskelion remains an enigmatic symbol, ripe for reinterpretation. As we delve into Scotland's Celtic heritage, it becomes clear that this emblematic motif is more than a relic of the past – it continues to inspire and intrigue us today, inviting us to unravel its hidden meanings.

Celestial Harmony Found

The triskelion's design is deeply rooted in astronomy, reflecting the celestial movements of the stars and constellations. In Celtic mythology, the triskelion was often associated with the great god Lugh, known for his exceptional archery skills. According to legend, Lugh created the world with three spearheads that held together the land, sky, and sea.

Archaeological findings suggest that the triskelion's design may have been inspired by ancient Celtic observations of the night sky. The Celts were skilled astronomers who closely followed the movements of the stars and constellations, often using them to predict seasonal changes and important agricultural events. The triskelion's three arms are thought to represent the celestial bodies that influenced their daily lives – the sun, moon, and stars.

Throughout Scotland's history, the triskelion has remained a powerful symbol of the nation's rich cultural heritage. Its enduring design has been incorporated into various art forms, from ancient Celtic knotwork to modern Scottish architecture. By studying the triskelion's astronomical roots, we can gain a deeper understanding of the intricate connections between Scotland's people, their environment, and the celestial world.

Triple Goddess Revealed

In the mystical realm of Celtic spirituality, the triskelion is often revered as a sacred emblem of the Triple Goddess. This ancient symbol has captivated the imagination of many, sparking an interest in its significance and cultural context. The triskelion's three interconnected arms are said to represent the feminine triple aspect: Maiden, Mother, and Crone.

According to Celtic mythology, the goddess embodied each of these aspects at different stages of her life cycle. As the Maiden, she represented youth and fertility; as the Mother, she symbolised nurturing and protection; and as the Crone, she embodied wisdom and mysticism. This multifaceted representation highlights the complexities and richness of feminine power in Celtic culture.

Throughout Scottish heritage, the triskelion has been used in various forms of art and symbolism, often associated with the goddess's triple aspect. Its enduring presence speaks to the deep reverence for the natural world and the cyclical rhythms of life, which are at the heart of Celtic spirituality.
